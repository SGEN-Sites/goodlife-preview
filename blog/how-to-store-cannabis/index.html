Lock it up. Keep it fresh.

Cool, dark, airtight and locked: how to keep every product fresh, and out of the wrong hands.

Guides·3 min read·Nov 18, 2026·By Good Life Collective

You picked it out, you paid for it — now keep it right. Good storage does two jobs: it keeps your products the way you bought them, and it keeps them away from anyone who shouldn't have them. The second job matters most, so we'll start there.

Safety first

For use only by adults 21 years of age and older. Keep out of reach of children and pets. In case of accidental ingestion or overconsumption, contact the Poison Center at 1-800-222-1222 or call 9-1-1.

Good Life Collective

That line runs at the bottom of every Good Life page, and it starts at home. Edibles and drinks look like everyday snacks and sodas, so every cannabis product belongs up high, out of sight and ideally locked.

  • Use a lockable box, cabinet or bag.
  • Keep products in their original packaging so the label stays with the product.
  • Never mix edibles in with regular snacks, or cannabis drinks in with regular cans.
  • Label anything homemade clearly — our cannabutter guide shows how.
  • Keep the Poison Center number handy: 1-800-222-1222.

Hosting? Do a sweep.

Before guests come over — especially anyone bringing kids — put everything away: jars, edibles, drinks, vapes, lighters and anything homemade. Pets count too. Keep edibles and infused butter where paws can't reach.

Cool, dark, dry and airtight

Heat, light, air and moisture are what wear cannabis down. Heat and light degrade cannabinoids and terpenes, too much air dries flower out, and too much moisture invites mold. So the rule is simple: somewhere cool, dark and dry, in an airtight container.

  • Flower: an airtight glass jar in a cool, dark cupboard. Skip spots that swing hot and cold or get steamy, like over the stove or in the bathroom.
  • Pre-rolls: sealed in their tubes or packs, away from heat.
  • Vapes: carts and all-in-ones hate heat, so keep them out of hot cars and direct sun.
  • Concentrates: sealed, cool and dark.
  • Edibles and drinks: in their original packaging, stored the way the package says, locked away.
  • Tinctures and topicals: caps on tight, somewhere cool and dark.

Keep different products in separate containers so smells don't mix, and label jars with what's inside and when you bought it, so the oldest gets used first.

Keep the smell in check

An airtight jar handles most of it, and it keeps the aroma — the terpenes behind it — where it belongs. For more, check your store's Accessories menu: when we wrote this, Canandaigua carried smell-proof bags and Astoria carried stash jars.

Build a stash kit

  • An airtight glass jar or two
  • A lockable box or bag
  • Labels and a marker for anything homemade
  • Your lighter, papers or battery, kept with the rest

Keep it all in one spot so nothing ends up loose around the house. Good Life lighters, cones and papers are in the merch shop.

When to let it go

Even well-stored cannabis doesn't last forever. Toss flower that smells musty or shows mold, edibles past any date on the package, and any cart or device that's leaking or damaged. When in doubt, don't.

On the move

Keep it sealed, keep it private and out of public view, and never drive impaired. And don't take cannabis across state lines — that's a federal matter, even between two legal states like New York and Oregon. Rules differ by state; our New York and Oregon law guides cover the basics. General information, not legal advice.

Buy what you'll use

Fresh beats stockpiled. Buy what you'll enjoy soon and come back for more — every Good Life store takes online orders for in-store pickup, and Rochester and Buffalo deliver. Keeping only what you'll use also means less to lock up. See pickup and delivery.

Quick answers

Should I keep flower in the fridge?

A cool, dark cupboard and an airtight jar do the job. For edibles and drinks, follow the package.

What should I do if a child gets into cannabis, or someone takes too much?

Contact the Poison Center at 1-800-222-1222 or call 9-1-1.

Can I take cannabis between New York and Oregon?

No. Don't take cannabis across state lines — federal law applies even between two legal states. This is general information, not legal advice.
